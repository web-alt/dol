              Ambedkar’s critique.

              Division of labour

              Division of labourers

              Ambedkar argued that dignity of labour is impossible under caste: occupations become hereditary and inescapable, tied to stigma, purity and pollution. For him the occupational hierarchy itself had to be annihilated, not merely the manual labour forced on oppressed groups idealised.

              Labour Day, 1 May.

              1886Chicago

              The Haymarket affair: workers striking for an eight-hour workday faced severe police violence. The international socialist and labour movements then chose 1 May to honour the victims and press for fair working conditions.

              The first formal, organised celebration of Labour Day in India, led by labour union leader Singaravelu Chettiar. Two meetings: at Triplicane beach and opposite the Madras High Court. The red flag was unfurled in India for the first time.

                    The law that tried to fix it.

                    0%of every Town Vending Committee must be street vendors themselves

                    The Street Vendors Act, 2014 formally recognises vendors’ occupational rights. Town Vending Committees survey vending areas, set zone capacity and issue vending certificates to prevent arbitrary eviction. One-third of the vendor representatives must be women. The law exists; turning it into everyday security is the challenge.

                                  When dignity is denied.

                                  Stigma. A case study. Rights respected and violated.

                                  Stigma: unequal respect for occupations.

                                    Traditionally labelled

                                      Stigma strips dignity and normalises exploitative working conditions.

                                      Relegated to marginalised Dalit and Bahujan communities, these workers are seen through a lens of inherited inferiority, not as citizens providing vital civic services. The lack of respect correlates with suppressed wages and abysmal working conditions.

                                      Case study: sanitation workers.

                                      Manual scavenging: the most severe and lethal violation of the dignity of labour in modern India.

                                      1993The 1993 Act

                                      Rights violated: Chennai.

                                      0days of hunger strike by Dalit sanitation workers in Chennai

                                      They protested arbitrary job dismissals and demanded permanent, secure employment. Workers testified to handling broken glass, needles and human excreta with bare hands, without adequate protective gear. Prolonged unemployment, mounting debt and hazardous conditions led to severe mental distress and tragic fatalities.

                                      The NAMASTE scheme (National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem) aims to mechanise all hazardous cleaning. By late 2025 it had validated over 88,448 workers and distributed over 83,000 PPE kits. Livelihood support, skill development and safety equipment: a tangible effort to restore occupational dignity.

                                          What recent government steps aim to change.

                                          For the first time, the law recognises gig and platform workers.

                                          The Code on Social Security, 2020 subsumes nine older labour laws into one framework. A Social Security Fund, partly financed by aggregator contributions of 1–2% of annual turnover, is to provide life insurance, disability cover, maternity benefits and health protection. The Code also universalizes Employees’ Provident Fund coverage and reduces gratuity eligibility for Fixed-Term Employees to one year.
